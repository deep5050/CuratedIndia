# Kailasanathar Temple

The Kanchi Kailasanathar Temple is the oldest structural temple in Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu, and is a premier example of early Dravidian architecture. Built around 700 CE by the Pallava King Narasimhavarman II (also known as Rajasimha) and completed by his son Mahendravarman III, the temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva.

**Note:** Known for its historical significance and exquisite Dravidian architecture, featuring intricate sandstone carvings.

[Google Maps link](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KailasanatharTemple)

Architectural Highlights
Sandstone & Granite: The foundation is built from solid granite to support the weight of the intricate sandstone superstructure.
Sub-Shrines: The inner courtyard is famous for its 58 small shrines built into the compound walls, each featuring unique carvings of Lord Shiva in various forms.
Sixteen-Sided Lingam: The main sanctum houses a massive, 16-sided Shiva Lingam made of black granite.
Lion Pillars: A signature of Pallava style, the temple features numerous pillars adorned with rearing lions (yalis).
Sculptures: Detailed bas-reliefs depict mythological scenes like Somaskanda (Shiva with Parvati and Skanda), Tripurantaka, and the cosmic dance of Shiva
